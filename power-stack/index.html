THE BIG STORY · AI CAPITAL CYCLE

Cloud demand is booming.
Cash flow is the real test.

Amazon's AWS sales accelerated 39%, yet trailing free cash flow fell to a $42.4bn outflow. Alphabet's quarterly operating cash flow was $42.3bn against $51.4bn in capital expenditure. Stronger sales do not guarantee higher cash available to shareholders when infrastructure is growing even faster. The question has shifted from “Is AI demand real?” to “Who earns enough to pay for it?”

DECISION QUEUE · SOURCE-LED

Where to focus the next research hour

01 / AMZN

Real cloud growth. Negative FCF.

AWS grew 39% to $46bn. Amazon plans approximately $220bn of 2026 cash capex. The test: will the next quarters show a rising incremental return on capacity?

Confirm cloud margin and FCF recover. Invalidate capex accelerates faster than operating cash.
Investigate Amazon ↗
02 / GOOGL

Cloud doubled. Obligations surged.

The 10-Q reports $924.4bn in material purchase commitments, plus $130.5bn of not-yet-commenced lease payments. Do not mistake backlog and commitments for free cash flow.

Confirm contracted capacity converts to profit. Invalidate cash funding/dilution outruns monetisation.
Investigate Alphabet ↗
03 / META

Advertising works. Spending bites.

Revenue +26%, yet GAAP operating margin is 20% after a $9.89bn legal charge. Excluding the charge improves the picture, but cannot fix −$2.12bn of reported FCF.

Confirm monetisation offsets AI investment. Invalidate sustained negative FCF despite ad growth.

MACRO TRANSMISSION · FOMC 28 OCT

Rates remain part of the AI bill.

The Fed held the funds target at 3.75–4.00% by a 10–2 vote, with both dissents preferring a hike. Cost of capital matters more when a company funds hundreds of billions of dollars in infrastructure. Higher financing costs can offset operating growth, even if AI demand is genuine.
