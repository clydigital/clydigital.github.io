CASH-CONVERSION TEST

AMZN — AWS is stronger, but shareholders are financing a capacity race

Amazon's Q3 revenue rose 12% to $201.9bn. AWS grew 39% to $46.0bn and delivered $17.8bn of segment operating income, up from $11.4bn a year earlier. Yet the reported trailing-twelve-month free cash flow fell from +$14.8bn to −$42.4bn.

AWS growth+39%
Operating income, Q3$28.1bn
TTM FCF−$42.4bn
2026 cash capex outlook≈$220bn

The bullish case

Cloud infrastructure demand is translating into actual paid revenue. AWS growth accelerated while Amazon's consolidated operating margin reached 13.9%. Management expects Q4 2026 sales between $240bn and $247bn, with operating income between $28.5bn and $33.5bn.

The problem

Operating cash flow over the trailing 12 months rose 29% to $168.3bn, but net cash purchases of property and equipment reached approximately $210.7bn. The resulting FCF shortfall is not a sign of collapsing customer demand; it is a sign that the infrastructure build absorbs cash faster than current operations produce it. Management expects 2027 infrastructure investment to rise meaningfully.

Investment review: WATCH / NOT YET SCORED

Confirm: AWS sustains strong profitable growth and the gap between operating cash flow and net infrastructure spending narrows.

Challenge: new capacity can become productive over several years, so a single negative FCF year is not automatically value destruction.

Invalidate: incremental spend rises faster than contracted demand, margins or cash collections for several quarters.

Missing before valuation: source-dated stock price, forward operating cash, useful-life assumptions and funding impact.

COMMITMENTS / FUNDING

GOOGL — 103% Cloud growth, but its commitments need an economic payoff

Alphabet reported $127.6bn in Q3 revenue (+25%) and $40.6bn operating income (+30%). Google Cloud revenue reached $30.7bn (+103%). Its 10-Q also shows quarterly operating cash of $42.3bn versus $51.4bn of capex: roughly −$9.1bn simple FCF.

Cloud revenue growth+103%
Operating margin31.8%
Q3 cash less PPE−$9.1bn
Material commitments$924.4bn

The contradiction

A fast-growing cloud backlog of $567.7bn shows commercial demand. It does not mean the cash has been received or that all contracts are equally profitable. Alphabet disclosed $924.4bn in material purchase commitments (of which $802.1bn relate to multiyear infrastructure, inventory, energy and content obligations), and $130.5bn of future payments for leases not yet commenced.

These obligations span years and have different legal/accounting treatment. Adding them into one “debt” number would overstate current borrowings. Yet they reveal that a major part of future operating cash must be earned before investors can expect more distributable cash.

The funding question

Alphabet reported $28.7bn in net proceeds from senior notes and $12.4bn from Class A/C share issuance during Q3. Those are genuine financing and dilution signals alongside unusually strong underlying operations, deserving more attention than a headline profit-growth figure alone.

Investment review: VERIFY CAPITAL RETURNS

Confirm: Cloud revenue/backlog convert to recurring operating cash flow, while margins, share issuance and future lease liabilities remain manageable.

Challenge: multi-year infrastructure commitments support multiple years of sales; they cannot all be compared with one quarter's cash flow.

Invalidate: repeated external funding and dilution without cash conversion or acceptable cloud margins.

Missing: full valuation, incremental cloud ROI and economic terms of future commitments.

ACCOUNTING / FCF QUALITY

META — the ad machine works; litigation and spending obscure the result

Meta posted Q3 revenue of $64.60bn, up 26%, while the average price per advertisement climbed 14%. GAAP operating income fell 37% to $12.99bn, but that figure includes a $9.89bn litigation charge. Free cash flow was negative $2.12bn despite operating cash flow of $32.79bn.

Revenue growth+26%
GAAP operating margin20%
Capex incl. lease principal$34.91bn
Quarterly FCF−$2.12bn

What the results really say

Removing the litigation charge yields about $22.88bn of operating income on management's stated reconciliation—much healthier than the unadjusted GAAP figure. That is a useful operating comparison, not permission to ignore the real cash cost or dismiss future legal risk.

Meta reported $82.45bn in cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities, so a negative FCF quarter is not synonymous with immediate distress. The concern is whether AI/research infrastructure and regulation consume too much of the growing advertising engine over several years.

Investment review: NORMALISE, THEN PRICE

Confirm: advertising pricing and impressions keep compounding, legal settlements do not recur at a similar scale, and capex normalises relative to operating cash.

Challenge: legal charges are non-operating in thesis terms but still economic costs; reported FCF should not be replaced with adjusted earnings.

Invalidate: recurring cash outflows outrun growth, or regulatory costs permanently compress margins.

Meta official Q3 release, 21 Oct 2026 ↗.

CASH QUALITY / BASELINE

MSFT — the comparatively stronger cash quarter has a capex catch

Microsoft's FY2027 Q1 (calendar Sep 2026 quarter) delivered $92.3bn revenue (+19%), Azure growth of 48% and $45.9bn operating income. Operating cash flow of $58.0bn less $39.367bn of cash additions to property and equipment produces approximately $18.633bn in simple free cash flow.

Azure revenue+48%
Operating income$45.9bn
Cash PPE$39.367bn
Simple quarter FCF+$18.633bn

Relative strength is not immunity

Microsoft's operating cash exceeded cash PP&E this quarter. That gives it a stronger contemporaneous cash cushion than the negative simple FCF quarters shown for Alphabet and Meta. However, those comparisons ignore differences in fiscal cycles, lease accounting, financing and capitalisation.

Azure's growth is a meaningful demand signal. The further valuation question remains: how much additional cash will required AI infrastructure absorb before the business can distribute more to shareholders?

Investment review: WATCH CAPITAL INTENSITY

Confirm: Azure revenue and profits scale faster than cash investment needs across subsequent quarters.

Challenge: one positive FCF quarter does not validate long-term AI returns.

Invalidate: worsening free cash flow and debt/lease obligations despite growing cloud sales.

Microsoft FY27 Q1 official release, 28 Oct 2026 ↗.

US RATE REGIME

Why the Fed's hold matters to this investment race

The FOMC maintained a 3.75%–4.00% target range on 28 October by a 10–2 vote; two members preferred raising rates. An AI company can report genuine demand growth and still create a poor shareholder return if the cost of financing rises while capacity remains underutilised.

The causal chain

High infrastructure requirements → debt/equity funding → interest/dilution burden → higher required future cash flow. The next source check is whether earnings and operating cash repay that financing, not simply whether revenue beats its last year.

Confirmation / invalidation

Confirm: cash conversion improves at the same time capital commitments moderate. Invalidate: rising borrowing, interest costs and dilution during softer cloud monetisation.

Federal Reserve FOMC statement, 28 Oct 2026 ↗.
